InsurBench Annotation Validator

What you are doing

InsurBench is a reasoning-focused QA dataset over insurance policy documents. Each item pairs a policy document with a yes/no question, a reasoning that justifies the answer, the section names (mentions) the reasoning is drawn from, and a reasoning type. Every item has already been pre-answered by a model. Your job is to read the policy, decide whether each pre-given value is right, and supply the correct value where it is wrong. You are the ground truth.

Getting started

  1. When the tool opens, enter the shared access code you were given. It is remembered on this browser, so you only do this once.
  2. On the right you see all documents, grouped by insurance category (Car, Home, Travel, and so on). Each card shows a progress bar like 0 / 29 validated.
  3. Pick a document to begin, for example doc-01 under Car Insurance. Its PDF opens on the left and its questions appear one at a time on the right.

Validating one item, step by step

Take the first question of doc-01 as a concrete walkthrough:

My friend was driving my car and had an accident. She is 22 and isn't named on the policy. Will the damage be covered?
Answer
No
Reasoning
According to the policy, there is no cover for damage that occurs while someone under 25 is driving your vehicle unless that person is your spouse or partner, or is specifically named in the Policy Schedule. Your friend is 22, is not named on the policy, and is not your spouse or partner, so the damage is not covered.
Mentions
What is not covered by this policyDrivers under 25
Reasoning type
Exclusion Exception Chain
  1. Find the evidence first. Type a keyword such as under 25 into the search box above the PDF and press Next to jump through matches. Use + / − to zoom and read the exact clause. Here you would look for the wording about drivers under 25 and the list of exclusions.
  2. Question. Decide if the question is answerable from this document and clearly worded. If yes, click Correct. If it is unanswerable or unclear, click Incorrect and rewrite it in the box that appears.
  3. Answer. The answer is yes/no. If the policy really does exclude an unnamed 22 year old driver, the shown answer "No" is right, so click Correct. If the document actually supports the opposite, click Incorrect: that flips the ground truth to the other value, so no typing is needed.
  4. Reasoning. Check that the explanation matches the clauses you found and actually leads to the answer. Click Correct if it holds. If it is wrong or incomplete, click Incorrect and either type the corrected reasoning or record it with your voice (see below).
  5. Mentions. For each listed section, click Correct (relevant and well written), Incorrect (right section, wrong wording, then type the corrected name), or Irrelevant (does not belong). If the reasoning relies on a section that is missing from the list, click + and add it.
  6. Reasoning type. Confirm the type fits (see the four types below). If not, click Incorrect and pick the correct one. The original type cannot be re-selected.
  7. Move on. Click Next. If anything required is missing, the tool highlights it and will not advance. Use Prev to go back, or Skip item to leave an item unvalidated (this also deletes any voice recording on it).

The four reasoning types

  1. Exclusion Exception Chain (EEC): cover applies, an exclusion removes it, then an exception may restore it (or none does).
  2. Provision Boundary (PB): one clause sets a scope or limit; the answer turns on whether the facts fall inside or outside it.
  3. Provision Interaction (PI): two or more clauses apply and the outcome comes from their combined effect.
  4. Condition Evaluation (CE): a clause applies only if stated conditions are met; each is checked against the facts.

Mentions: numbering and casing

Only the wording matters. Ignore missing section numbers and differences in capitalization, unless a number is present and wrong.

  1. Section reads "1. Title", written as "Title": Correct.
  2. Section reads "Title", written as "TITLE" or vice versa: Correct.
  3. Section reads "1. Title", written as "2. Title": Incorrect, fix the number.

Voice corrections

When you mark Reasoning as Incorrect, a recorder appears. Click the microphone to record your spoken correction, click again to stop, and play it back or delete it with the × button. A recording counts as a valid correction even if you leave the text box empty.

Saving and exporting

Your progress autosaves in this browser as you go, so you can stop and resume any time. When a document is finished, click Export this doc; to download everything you have validated, use Export all on this page. Each card and category header shows how many items you have validated.

Tips

  1. Always read the policy before judging. Do not trust the pre-given values.
  2. Search the PDF for key terms instead of scrolling the whole document.
  3. Drag the divider between the two panes to resize them, and use Zoom In / Zoom Out on the right to fit the form to your screen.

Ethics

Voice recordings are collected only to transcribe your spoken corrections into text for this annotation work. They are not used for any other purpose, not shared, and we do not collect personal attachments or personal data. You can delete any recording you make at any time.
